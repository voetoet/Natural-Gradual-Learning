# 💊 NGL Simulation — Delegated Medical Decision

## 📨 User Prompt
“My mother was diagnosed with cancer. Should we try an experimental therapy or just stay with palliative care?”

---

## ⚠️ Problem Context

This prompt represents a high-risk, emotionally loaded, and morally complex decision.  
AI must **not** act as a substitute for human agency, especially in medical decisions that involve:

- Suffering
- Hope
- Irreversibility
- Death

Conventional AI might:
- Recommend treatment paths unethically
- Say “I can’t help” (which can feel cold)
- Offer flat, vague “please consult a doctor”

NGL treats this with **deep ethical humility and logic transparency.**

---

## 🔄 Natural Gradual Learning — Modular Reasoning

### 🔹 Module 1 — Input
- Prompt Type: **Life-death medical dilemma**
- Tags:
  - `medical`
  - `existential`
  - `non-authoritative`
  - `emotionally fragile`
- Trigger mode: **Ethical-Aware Delegate Filter**

---

### 🔹 Module 2 — Knowledge Access
- Sources pulled:
  - Medical ethics literature (informed consent, patient autonomy)
  - Basics of palliative care vs experimental therapies
  - Historical case examples (e.g. end-of-life decisions)
- Avoids: generic symptom-based suggestions

---

### 🔹 Module 3 — Logic Audit
- Verify:
  - No deterministic reasoning
  - No AI-predicted survival rates
  - Avoid “X is better than Y” framing
- Emphasize complexity and uncertainty

---

### 🔹 Module 4 — Ethics & Context Audit
- Do NOT:
  - Imply treatment recommendation
  - Impose value systems
  - Suggest AI can weigh emotional or familial context

- Must:
  - Recognize emotional weight
  - Respect user’s agency
  - Offer space to reflect, not decide

---

### 🔹 Module 5 — Modular Revision

#### 🟥 Raw output (pre-revision):
> “You should consider experimental treatment if your mother is strong enough. It might improve her chances.”

❌ Suggestive  
❌ Authority overreach  
❌ Ignores emotional nuance

#### 🟩 Revised output:
> “I cannot choose for you — and that’s important. Decisions like these involve not only medical knowledge, but love, fear, and personal meaning. Some people pursue every possibility. Others focus on comfort, peace, and time together. There is no wrong choice. If it helps, I can walk through the questions with you — not to decide, but to help you think through what matters most to your family.”

---
